# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Festival directors & programmers** — curating submissions, managing judging panels, running award ceremonies, hosting live screening events. They want a platform that matches the seriousness and prestige of what they do.

**Filmmakers** — submitting work, tracking status, uploading screeners, joining live Q&As and screening rooms. They want to feel like their work is being treated with respect, not processed like a form.

Both sides use the platform daily. The tone must work for industry insiders on both ends.

## Product Purpose

FestScout is open-source film festival management software (AGPL-3.0) you self-host: submissions, judging, payments, awards, and a built-in live video system (screening rooms, filmmaker Q&As, panel discussions). The video system is the primary differentiator over FilmFreeway and similar tools: festivals can run entirely inside FestScout, from first submission to live closing night ceremony.

Success looks like: a festival director who never has to leave FestScout, and a filmmaker who feels their work is seen by people who care about cinema. The `marketing/` site is for public product storytelling; each deployed instance has its own local homepage for sign-in and account creation.

## Brand Personality

**Cinematic. Insider. Uncompromising.**

The platform for people who take film seriously. Not corporate, not startup-cheerful, not creative-agency-whimsical. The aesthetic of a well-run international film festival — dark, editorial, high-contrast, confident. Feels like the platform was built by people who actually go to festivals.

## Anti-references

- **FilmFreeway**: Clean but corporate. Feels like submitting tax forms, not films. Generic blue SaaS with no cultural identity.
- **Startup landing page templates**: Hero stat blocks ("10,000+ festivals"), feature icon grids, testimonial carousels, the AI scaffold. Zero.
- **Overly dark / gamer aesthetic**: Dark mode that's edgy for its own sake, neon overload, RGB vibes. This is film, not esports.
- **Generic purple SaaS**: The current FestScout site — bland purple/blue gradients, glassmorphism cards, gradient text. Nothing distinctive.

## Design Principles

1. **Cinema over software** — every surface should feel like it belongs in a festival catalog or a Criterion blu-ray, not a SaaS dashboard. Film culture has its own visual language; use it.
2. **Both sides of the table** — the platform serves organizers and filmmakers equally. Never make a filmmaker feel like they're filling out a bureaucratic form.
3. **Video is the edge** — the built-in live system (screening rooms, Q&As) is what no one else has. Feature it like a lead character, not a footnote.
4. **Confidence without noise** — big type, strong contrast, considered negative space. No decorative gradients, no glassmorphism, no pattern-for-pattern's-sake.
5. **Earned credibility** — copy names what the product does, not how it feels. No "empower", no "seamless", no "next-generation". Festival people are skeptical; earn trust by being specific.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. High contrast is baked into the cinematic aesthetic — dark backgrounds with bright text naturally pushes contrast ratios up. Reduced motion must be respected; no motion-gated content. All interactive elements keyboard-navigable.
